I · The course
01 / 05

A figure-eight through the oaks.

Nicklaus routed May River as two counter-rotating loops — front nine clockwise through the forest, back nine counter-clockwise along the water. The river appears on five holes, the marsh on many more, and the course never repeats a sightline.

There are no cliffs here. Nicklaus defends par with gentle doglegs, risk-reward carries, and runoffs — not elevation. The fairways are wide enough to breathe on, but paspalum is hot off the turf and rejects very little spin, so mid-irons bite hard and bad yardage is punished immediately. Miss the middle of a green and you're playing a different game: collection areas pull balls into awkward chipping lies, and every recovery asks whether you trust your hands on grain.

What the course really asks — on almost every hole — is which half of the green you want to play to. Nicklaus greens here are turtle-backed, domed, tilted. Pin position changes the hole more than wind does. The best caddie advice you can get is rarely about club selection. It's about which side of the flag is safe.

The atmosphere does real work. Live oaks frame almost every tee box. The May River glints through the trees. The air holds salt and pine. It is a place before it is a test — and then, hole 9, hole 15, hole 18, it becomes a test.

Caddie's opening read Pick the right tees. Oak (7,171) is a scratch ask; most 10-handicaps play from Hickory (6,065) and shoot closer to their number. Greens run 10–11.5 Stimp, faster in morning. Stay below the hole every time. Always.

IV · The verdict

What May River asks of you.

Three things every player should pack mentally before the first tee.

I
Stay below the hole
Greens are paspalum, fast, and tilted. Below the hole every time. A 30-foot uphill putt is better than a 10-foot downhill on this property.
II
Pick the right tees
Hickory (6,065) is the right tee for a 10-handicap. Oak (7,171) plays brutally long with the swirling river wind. Ego is the enemy.
III
Use a caddie
